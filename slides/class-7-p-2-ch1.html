Replicating a Shape: Are Arm Lengths Enough?

🔍 The Signboard Problem

A tick mark symbol on a roadside signboard needs to be recreated accurately on a new board.

  • Tracing Method: Works for small drawings, but difficult or impossible on large signboards and physical structures.
  • Measuring Arm Lengths: Suppose the arms measure $AB = 4\text{ cm}$ and $BC = 8\text{ cm}$.
  • Crucial Observation: Several completely different symbols can be drawn with these exact arm lengths simply by varying the opening!
📐 Fixing the Angle $\angle ABC$

Arm lengths alone are not sufficient. To lock in the exact replica, we must specify the angle:

$AB = 4\text{ cm},\; BC = 8\text{ cm},\; \angle ABC = 80^\circ$

These 3 measurements uniquely fix both the shape and the size.

✦ Section 1.1 • Core Definition Pages 2–3

The Meaning of Congruence

✨ Exact Geometric Twins

Figures that are exact copies of each other — having both the same shape and the same size — are called congruent.

$\text{Congruent} \iff \text{Superimpose exactly one over the other}$
  • If you trace one figure on tracing paper, it will cover the other figure completely with no gaps or overlaps.
  • Every point on the first boundary coincides with a unique point on the second.
🔄 Rigid Motions: Rotation & Reflection

Key Textbook Principle: While testing congruence, a figure can be rotated or flipped (reflected) before superimposing it!

Orientation does not change congruence. A teardrop pointing left is congruent to the same teardrop pointing right.

Replicating a Triangular Frame (Meera & Rabia)

👭 The Classroom Scenario

Meera and Rabia are tasked with creating an identical cardboard cutout of a large triangular frame in their school.

  • The frame is too large to trace directly onto paper.
  • Using a measuring tape, they measure the three sides: $40\text{ cm},\; 60\text{ cm},\; 80\text{ cm}$.
  • Rabia reaches for her protractor to record all 3 angles.
  • Meera stops her: "The angles are not required! With the three side lengths alone, we can create an exact congruent twin."
🤔 Rabia's Compass Doubt

Suppose we construct a scaled base $AB = 6\text{ cm}$, then draw circles of radius $4\text{ cm}$ from $A$ and $8\text{ cm}$ from $B$.

The circles intersect at two points: $E$ above and $F$ below, forming $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle ABF$. Are these two triangles congruent?

✦ Criterion 1 • SSS Condition Page 6

The SSS (Side-Side-Side) Congruence Condition

📜 The SSS Congruence Theorem

If two triangles have the same three sidelengths, then they are guaranteed to be congruent.

🔍 Why Rabia's Two Triangles are Congruent
  • In the circular arc construction, $AB$ acts as an exact line of symmetry.
  • Folding along the segment $AB$ brings point $E$ directly onto point $F$.
  • Both $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle ABF$ have sides $6\text{ cm},\; 4\text{ cm},$ and $8\text{ cm}$.
  • Hence, Meera was completely right: the side lengths uniquely fix the triangle!
📐 Formal SSS Condition

For any two triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle XYZ$:

$$\begin{cases} AB = XY \\ BC = YZ \\ CA = ZX \end{cases} \implies \triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$$

Once all three pairs of corresponding sides are verified equal, all corresponding angles are automatically forced to be equal!

✦ Mathematical Notation Pages 6–7

Conventions: Vertex Order Matters!

🎯 The 1-to-1 Correspondence Rule

When we write $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$, the order of letters specifies exact superimposition:

$$\begin{aligned} A &\longleftrightarrow X \\ B &\longleftrightarrow Y \\ C &\longleftrightarrow Z \end{aligned}$$
  • Equal Sides: $AB = XY,\; BC = YZ,\; AC = XZ$
  • Equal Angles: $\angle A = \angle X,\; \angle B = \angle Y,\; \angle C = \angle Z$
⚠️ Common Naming Mistake

It is mathematically incorrect to write:

$\triangle ACB \cong \triangle XYZ \quad \text{❌ (Mismatched!)}$

Because that would falsely imply side $AC = XY$ and $\angle C = \angle Y$.

✅ Correct alternative naming: $\triangle ACB \cong \triangle XZY$ or $\triangle BAC \cong \triangle YXZ$.

✦ Textbook Figure Analysis Pages 7–9

Applying SSS: Rectangles & Kite Quadrilaterals

📐 Diagonal of Rectangle $ABCD$

Diagonal $BD$ divides the rectangle into two triangles: $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle CDB$.

  • $AB = CD$ (opposite sides of rectangle)
  • $AD = CB$ (opposite sides of rectangle)
  • $BD = DB$ (shared common side)

By SSS condition: $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle CDB$.

Notice: Overlapping $A \to C,\; B \to D,\; D \to B$. (Not $B \to B$!)

🪁 Kite Quadrilateral ($AB=AD, CB=CD$)

Join diagonal $AC$. In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ADC$:

  • $AB = AD$ (given equal adjacent sides)
  • $CB = CD$ (given equal adjacent sides)
  • $AC = AC$ (shared common diagonal)
$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle ADC \quad \text{[By SSS]}$

Immediate deduction: $\angle BAC = \angle DAC$ and $\angle BCA = \angle DCA$. Thus, the diagonal $AC$ bisects both angles $\angle BAD$ and $\angle BCD$!

✦ Angle Analysis Pages 9–10

Measuring the Angles: Why AAA Fails

🔍 What If We Measure All 3 Angles?

Suppose Meera and Rabia measured the three angles of the frame:

$$\angle A = 80^\circ,\quad \angle B = 30^\circ,\quad \angle C = 70^\circ$$

Can an exact duplicate of the frame be guaranteed from these three angle measurements?

  • Sum of angles is $80^\circ + 30^\circ + 70^\circ = 180^\circ$.
  • We can easily construct a tiny triangle on a sheet of paper with these angles.
  • We can also construct a giant triangular roof or billboard with these exact same angles!
⚠️ The AAA Limitation

These triangles have the same shape, but different sizes!

$\text{AAA condition does NOT guarantee congruence!}$

Two triangles that have the same set of three angles are similar, but not necessarily congruent. To ensure identical size, at least one linear dimension (side length) must be locked in.

✦ Criterion 2 • SAS Condition Page 10

The SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Condition

📜 The SAS Congruence Theorem

If two sides and the included angle of one triangle are equal to two sides and the included angle of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

📌 What is an "Included Angle"?

The included angle is the angle formed between the two specified arms:

  • For sides $AB$ and $AC$, the included angle is $\angle A$.
  • For sides $BC$ and $BA$, the included angle is $\angle B$.
$$\begin{cases} AB = XY = 6\text{ cm} \\ AC = XZ = 5\text{ cm} \\ \angle A = \angle X = 30^\circ \end{cases} \implies \triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$$
✨ Why SAS Works Uniquely

Once arms $AB$ and $AC$ are drawn with angle $\angle A$ fixed, endpoints $B$ and $C$ are pinned in space.

There is only one unique straight line segment connecting $B$ and $C$. Therefore, the third side $BC$ and both remaining angles $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ are rigidly fixed.

✦ Counterexample Analysis Pages 10–12

Why SSA (Non-Included Angle) Fails!

🔍 The Construction Experiment

Suppose $PQ = 6\text{ cm}$, $\angle P = 30^\circ$, and side $QR = 4\text{ cm}$ (opposite to $\angle P$, non-included):

  • Step 1: Draw base $PQ = 6\text{ cm}$.
  • Step 2: Draw ray $l$ from $P$ at $30^\circ$.
  • Step 3: With center $Q$, swing an arc of radius $4\text{ cm}$.

The circular arc cuts ray $l$ at TWO distinct points: $R$ and $S$!

⚡ The Two Ambiguous Triangles

Both $\triangle PQR$ (obtuse) and $\triangle PQS$ (acute) have $PQ=6\text{ cm}$, $QR=QS=4\text{ cm}$, and $\angle P=30^\circ$.

$\text{SSA does NOT guarantee congruence!}$

✦ Criteria 3 & 4 • ASA & AAS Pages 12–16

The ASA & AAS Congruence Conditions

📜 ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)

When two angles and the included side are equal:

$$\begin{cases} \angle B = \angle Y = 50^\circ \\ BC = YZ = 5\text{ cm} \\ \angle C = \angle Z = 30^\circ \end{cases} \implies \triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$$

Fixing the base length and drawing rays at the given interior angles forces them to intersect at one unique vertex point.

📜 AAS (Angle-Angle-Side)

What if the equal side is non-included?

  • Sum of angles in any triangle is always $180^\circ$.
  • If $\angle A = \angle X = 35^\circ$ and $\angle C = \angle Z = 75^\circ$, then:
$\angle B = 180^\circ - (35^\circ + 75^\circ) = 70^\circ = \angle Y$

Conclusion: Knowing two angles always determines the third. AAS immediately converts into ASA, so AAS guarantees congruence!

✦ Criterion 5 • RHS Condition Pages 16–17

The RHS (Right-Hypotenuse-Side) Condition

📜 The RHS Congruence Theorem

Two right-angled triangles are congruent if the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and corresponding side of the other.

📐 The 3 Components of RHS
  • R (Right Angle): $\angle B = \angle Q = 90^\circ$
  • H (Hypotenuse): Side opposite to the right angle ($AC = PR = 5\text{ cm}$)
  • S (Any One Side): $BC = QR = 4\text{ cm}$
$$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR \quad \text{[By RHS]}$$
🔍 Why RHS Succeeds Where SSA Failed

RHS is technically a special case of SSA (Right angle is opposite the hypotenuse).

Because the perpendicular ray goes straight up at $90^\circ$, an arc of radius $5\text{ cm}$ from $R$ cuts the ray in only one positive location above base $QR$.

(The downward intersection merely produces an upside-down congruent twin.)

✦ Section 1.3 • Triangle Properties Pages 17–19

Angles of Isosceles & Equilateral Triangles

🔺 Isosceles Triangle Theorem

In $\triangle ABC$, let $AB = AC$. Draw altitude $AD \perp BC$:

  • In right $\triangle ADB$ and $\triangle ADC$:
  • $AB = AC$ (hypotenuses are equal, given)
  • $AD = AD$ (common leg)
  • $\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90^\circ$

By RHS: $\triangle ADB \cong \triangle ADC \implies \angle B = \angle C$.

$\text{Angles opposite to equal sides are equal!}$
🔺 Equilateral Triangle Deduction

In equilateral $\triangle ABC$, all three sides are equal: $AB = BC = CA$.

  • Since $AB = AC \implies \angle B = \angle C$.
  • Since $AB = BC \implies \angle C = \angle A$.
  • Therefore: $\angle A = \angle B = \angle C$.
$$3 \times (\text{angle}) = 180^\circ \implies \text{Each angle} = 60^\circ$$

Just using congruence, we prove every equilateral triangle has three $60^\circ$ angles!

Congruent Triangles in Architecture & Design

🏛️ Louvre Pyramid & Giza

Louvre Pyramid (Paris) & Pyramids of Giza (Egypt): The four triangular faces are congruent isosceles triangles. Standardized glass panes and stone blocks form identical structural units.

🌉 Howrah Bridge (Kolkata)

Rabindra Setu (Howrah Bridge): Truss bridge structures rely entirely on repeating congruent triangles because triangles are rigid shapes that distribute loads without deforming.

✨ Geodesic Domes & Rangoli

Domes & Traditional Rangoli: Modular tessellations of congruent equilateral and isosceles triangles create spherical domes and symmetric 8-pointed star patterns.

✦ Exercise Highlights Pages 20–21

Representative Exercise Solutions

📝 Problem 1: Transversal Proof

Given: $OB = OC$ and $OA = OD$. Prove $AB \parallel CD$.

• Vertically opposite: $\angle AOB = \angle DOC$
• By SAS: $\triangle AOB \cong \triangle DOC$
• CPCTC: $\angle OAB = \angle ODC$
• Alternate interior angles are equal:

$\implies AB \parallel CD$
📝 Problem 2: Center of Circle

Given: $A$ is center of circle, chord $BC$, $\angle BAC = 120^\circ$. Find $\angle B, \angle C$.

• $AB = AC = \text{Radius } r$
• Therefore $\triangle ABC$ is isosceles: $\angle B = \angle C$
• Angle sum: $120^\circ + 2\angle B = 180^\circ$

$\angle B = \angle C = 30^\circ$
📝 Problem 3: Square Congruence

Given: Square $ABCD$. Is $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle ADC$ and $\triangle CDA$?

• $AB = AD$, $BC = DC$, $AC = AC$ $\implies \triangle ABC \cong \triangle ADC$ (SSS)
• In fact, due to symmetry, $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$ as well!

$\text{Congruent in } \mathbf{2} \text{ different ways!}$

✦ Chapter Summary Page 22

Chapter 1 Recap: Key Takeaways

✅ Congruence Fundamentals
  • Congruent Figures: Identical in shape and size; superimpose exactly over each other.
  • Rigid Motions: Rotation and reflection (flipping) are allowed when testing congruence.
  • Vertex Correspondence: Writing $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$ requires matching equal sides and angles in exact order.
  • Non-guarantee Cases: AAA and SSA do NOT guarantee triangle congruence.
✅ Valid Congruence Criteria & Theorems
  • SSS: Three pairs of corresponding sides are equal.
  • SAS: Two sides and the included angle are equal.
  • ASA & AAS: Two angles and any side (included or non-included) are equal.
  • RHS: Right angle, hypotenuse, and one corresponding leg are equal.
  • Isosceles Property: Angles opposite to equal sides in a triangle are equal.
  • Equilateral Property: All three angles in any equilateral triangle measure $60^\circ$.
