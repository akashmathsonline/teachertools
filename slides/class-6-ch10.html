🛗 The Lift Mechanism
  • The lift has two simple buttons: $+$ (go up) and $-$ (go down).
  • Pressing $+$ twice moves up 2 floors: written as $+2$ (or $++$).
  • Pressing $-$ twice moves down 2 floors: written as $-2$ (or $--$).
✨ Defining Positive & Negative Numbers

• Positive Number: Number with a $+$ sign (or none). Represents floors above ground.
• Negative Number: Number with a $-$ sign. Represents floors below ground.
• Zero ($0$): The reference ground floor. Zero is neither positive nor negative!

📌 Fundamental Movement Formula
$$\mathbf{\text{Starting Floor} + \text{Movement} = \text{Target Floor}}$$
Example 1: Going Up

Start at Food Court ($+1$) and press $+2$ in the lift:
$(+1) + (+2) = +3$ (Book Store).

$$\text{Start } (+1) \xrightarrow{\;+2\;} \text{Target } (+3)$$
Example 2: Going Down

Start at Art Centre ($+2$) and press $-3$ in the lift:
$(+2) + (-3) = -1$ (Toy Store).

$$\text{Start } (+2) \xrightarrow{\;-3\;} \text{Target } (-1)$$
🔄 Combining Button Presses is also Addition

Gurmit was in the Toy Store ($-1$). He pressed $+$ twice, then realized his error and pressed $-$ three times:
Net Movement: $(+2) + (-3) = -1$ (One floor below where he started).

Concept Focus Back to Zero & Additive Inverses

Cancelling Out to Zero

On the ground floor ($0$), Basant accidentally presses $+3$. To cancel it and stay on the ground floor, he must press $-3$!

$$(+3) + (-3) = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad (-2) + (+2) = 0$$
Definition: Additive Inverse

Every integer has an additive inverse (or opposite). When an integer is added to its inverse, the sum is always zero.

$$a + (-a) = 0$$
Examples of Pairs
  • The inverse of $+4$ is $-4$
  • The inverse of $-7$ is $+7$
  • The inverse of $-9$ is $+9$
  • The inverse of $0$ is $0$ (itself!)

Ordering Integers Comparing Numbers Using Floors
Pages 246–247
Higher vs Lower Floors

Floor $+4$ is higher than Floor $+3$, so $+4 > +3$ (or $+3 < +4$).
What about negative floors? Floor $-3$ is higher than Floor $-4$, because Floor $-4$ is further deep underground!

$$-4 < -3 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -3 > -4$$
Key Ordering Principles
  • All positive numbers are greater than 0 ($+1 > 0$).
  • All negative numbers are less than 0 ($-1 < 0$).
  • Any positive number is greater than any negative number.
  • For two negative numbers: the one with the larger numeral is smaller (further down).
Comparison Examples
  • $-2 < +5$ (negative is below positive)
  • $-5 < -3$ ($-5$ is deeper down)
  • $-10 > -12$ ($-10$ is higher than $-12$)
  • $0 > -20$ (ground is above basement)
  • $-25 < -7$

Subtraction as "Finding the Missing Quantity"

Earlier subtraction was "take away" ($10 - 4 = 6$). In integers, subtraction means finding the change needed to reach a target:

$$\mathbf{\text{Target Floor} - \text{Starting Floor} = \text{Movement Needed}}$$
Case 1: Upward Move

Start: $+2$ (Art Centre)
Target: $+5$ (Sports Centre)
Movement:

$$(+5) - (+2) = +3$$

Press $+3$ (3 floors up).

Case 2: Downward Move

Start: $+3$ (Books)
Target: $-1$ (Toys)
Movement:

$$(-1) - (+3) = -4$$

Press $-4$ (4 floors down).

Case 3: From Negative Floor

Start: $-2$ (Video Games)
Target: $+2$ (Art Centre)
Movement:

$$(+2) - (-2) = +4$$

Press $+4$ (4 floors up).

⛏️ The Deep Mine Model

Minerals exist above and below ground level ($0\,\text{m}$):
• Upper tunnels: $+40\,\text{m},\; +100\,\text{m},\; +125\,\text{m},\; +180\,\text{m}$
• Lower shafts: $-50\,\text{m},\; -80\,\text{m},\; -125\,\text{m},\; -175\,\text{m}$

$$(+40) - (-50) = +90\,\text{m} \quad(\text{move up } 90\,\text{m})$$

To move from $-50\,\text{m}$ to $+40\,\text{m}$, lift rises $50\,\text{m}$ to ground, then $40\,\text{m}$ more!

🚀 The Infinite Lift Concept

We don't need a real building — imagine an infinite lift moving endlessly upwards ($+1, +2, +3 \dots$) and downwards ($-1, -2, -3 \dots$).

$$(+2000) - (-200) = +2200$$

Notice: $(+2000) + (+200)$ also equals $+2200$.
Golden Rule: Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding the corresponding positive number!

Definition of Integers: $\dots, -4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, \dots$

Sketching an Unmarked Number Line

For large numbers, you do not need every tick mark. Just sketch a line, mark $0$, and draw arrows indicating jumps!

$$85 + (-60) = 25 \quad\text{(Start at } 85\text{, jump } 60\text{ left } \longrightarrow \text{land at } 25\text{)}$$ $$(-100) - (+250) = -350 \quad(250 + ? = -100 \implies ? = -350)$$
Convert Subtraction to Addition

Replace the number being subtracted with its additive inverse, and add!

$$a - b = a + (-b)$$

• $(+7) - (+5) = (+7) + (-5) = 2$
• $(+8) - (-2) = (+8) + (+2) = 10$

Convert Addition to Subtraction

Similarly, adding an integer is the same as subtracting its additive inverse!

$$a + b = a - (-b)$$

• $9 + (-6) = 9 - 6 = 3$
• $(-3) + (-2) = -3 - 2 = -5$

Section 10.2 Subtraction Using Tokens for Subtraction
Pages 257–259
Simple Case: Enough Tokens Present

$(+5) - (+4)$: Take away 4 positive tokens from 5 positives $\implies \mathbf{+1}$.

$(-7) - (-5)$: Take away 5 negative tokens from 7 negatives $\implies \mathbf{-2}$.

$$(-7) - (-5) = -2$$
The Clever Trick: Introducing Zero Pairs!

What if you need to subtract tokens that aren't there?
Calculate: $(+4) - (-6)$

  • Start with 4 positives: $\oplus\,\oplus\,\oplus\,\oplus$
  • Need to remove 6 negatives, but there are none!
  • Solution: Add 6 zero pairs (value = 0).
  • Now remove the 6 negatives!
  • You are left with $4 + 6 = \mathbf{+10}$!
Subtracting a Positive from a Negative: $(-3) - (+5)$

Start with 3 negatives. To take away 5 positives, introduce 5 zero pairs. Remove the 5 positives. Left with $3 + 5 = 8$ negatives $\implies \mathbf{-8}$.

Section 10.3 Real-World Integers: Money & Elevation
Pages 259–261
💰 Credits and Debits

In banking and passbooks:
• Credits (Deposits): Positive numbers ($+$)
• Debits (Payments/Withdrawals): Negative numbers ($-$)

$$\text{Balance} = \sum \text{Credits} - \sum \text{Debits}$$

If balance goes below zero (e.g., $-\text{₹}20$), it means an overdraft/debt!

🏔️ Geographical Cross Sections

Reference baseline is Sea Level = $0\,\text{m}$:
• Mountains/hills: Positive elevations ($+1500\,\text{m}$)
• Trenches/ocean floors: Negative depths ($-1200\,\text{m}$)

$$\text{Mt. Everest: } +8848\,\text{m} \quad\mid\quad \text{Mariana: } -10994\,\text{m}$$

Challenger Deep is the lowest known point on Earth!

🌡️ Temperature: Measured in Celsius ($^\circ\text{C}$). $0^\circ\text{C}$ is the freezing point of water. Hot summer days rise to $+45^\circ\text{C}$, while winter in Leh/Ladakh drops below zero to $-4^\circ\text{C}$ or $-15^\circ\text{C}$!

🔲 Hollow Integer Grid (Border Sum)

In a $3 \times 3$ hollow frame, every outer row and column adds up to the identical number called the Border Sum!

[ 4 ] [ -1 ] [ -3 ]
[-3 ] [ ] [ 1 ]
[-1 ] [ -1 ] [ 2 ]

Top row: $4 + (-1) + (-3) = \mathbf{0}$
Left col: $4 + (-3) + (-1) = \mathbf{0}$
Border sum $= 0$!

✨ Amazing Number Grid Game

Follow these simple steps on a $4 \times 4$ grid:

  1. Circle any number.
  2. Strike out its entire row and column.
  3. Circle any remaining unstruck number.
  4. Repeat until 4 numbers are circled.
$$\text{Sum of circled numbers is ALWAYS constant!}$$

No matter which numbers you pick, the sum never changes!

Section 10.5 A Pinch of History — Ancient Roots of Integers
Pages 266–268
📜 Ancient Accounting

• China (c. 1st–2nd c. CE): Nine Chapters on Mathematical Art used red and black rods for positive & negative numbers.

• India (c. 300 BCE): Kautilya's Arthaśhāstra recognized negative account balances.

• Bakhshālī Manuscript (c. 300 CE): Marked negative numbers with a special symbol.

👑 Brahmagupta (628 CE) — Brāhma-sphuṭa-siddhānta

Brahmagupta gave the first general mathematical treatment treating positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero on an equal footing—forming what modern mathematicians call a ring!

• Positive $+$ Positive $=$ Positive ($2 + 3 = 5$)
• Negative $+$ Negative $=$ Negative ($(-2) + (-3) = -5$)
• Number $+$ Inverse $= 0$ ($a + (-a) = 0$)
• Subtracting a negative is adding a positive: $a - (-b) = a + b$

European mathematicians took over 1,000 years to accept them; in the 18th century, Lazare Carnot still called negatives "absurd"!

Interactive Game Integers: Snakes and Ladders
Page 271
🎲 Rules of the Game
  • Players & Pawns: 2 players, 1 pawn each. Both start at $0$.
  • Winning Goal: First player to reach either $+50$ OR $-50$ wins!
  • Two Dice:
    • Die 1 has numbers $+1$ to $+6$
    • Die 2 has numbers $-1$ to $-6$
  • Strategic Choice: After each roll, you can add or subtract the two numbers in any order!
  • Positive total moves towards $+50$; negative total moves towards $-50$.
🧠 Tactical Example

Suppose you roll $+4$ and $-6$:

• Option A (Add): $(+4) + (-6) = \mathbf{-2}$ (Move 2 steps toward $-50$)
• Option B (Subtract): $(+4) - (-6) = +4 + 6 = \mathbf{+10}$ (Move 10 steps toward $+50$!)
• Option C (Subtract): $(-6) - (+4) = \mathbf{-10}$ (Move 10 steps toward $-50$!)

Notice how subtracting a negative number gives a huge positive surge!

Problem 1: Sequences

Question: Complete the pattern:
$(-40), (-34), (-28), (-22), \dots$

$$\text{Step: Add } +6$$ $$-22 + 6 = \mathbf{-16}$$ $$-16 + 6 = \mathbf{-10}$$ $$-10 + 6 = \mathbf{-4}$$

Answer: $-16, -10, -4$

Problem 2: Card Target

Cards: $(+1), (+7), (+18), (-5), (-2), (-9)$.
Make an expression closest to $-30$.

$$(-2) + (-9) - (+18) - (+1)$$ $$= -11 - 18 - 1 = \mathbf{-30}$$

Result: Exactly $-30$!

Problem 3: Token String

Question: String of 100 tokens with repeating blocks of $(+++)$ and $(--)$. Value?

$$\text{Block: } 3 - 2 = +1 \text{ (per 5 tokens)}$$ $$100 / 5 = 20 \text{ blocks}$$ $$\text{Value} = 20 \times 1 = \mathbf{20}$$

Answer: $+20$

Chapter Summary Core Takeaways & Rules Checklist
Pages 269–270
✅ Concept Recap
  • Integers: $\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$
  • Zero ($0$): Neither positive nor negative. The center reference.
  • Additive Inverse: $a + (-a) = 0$. The opposite number.
  • Order on Number Line: Left is smaller, right is greater ($a < b$).
  • Zero Pair: $\oplus + \ominus = 0$. Can be added/removed freely.
✅ Operations Checklist
  • Movement Rule: $\text{Start} + \text{Movement} = \text{Target}$
  • Difference Rule: $\text{Target} - \text{Start} = \text{Movement}$
  • Subtraction to Addition: $a - b = a + (-b)$
  • Subtracting Negative: $a - (-b) = a + b$
  • Real-world models: Lifts, mineshafts, banking (credits/debits), temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$), sea level.
