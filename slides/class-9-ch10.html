When Averages Collide: The Badminton Academy

Why averaging two averages directly can lead to false conclusions

✕
Method 1: The Simple Average Trap
  • Seniors ($n=8$): Average height $= 165.5\text{ cm}$
  • Juniors ($m=3$): Average height $= 149.33\text{ cm}$
  • Naive average calculation:
    $$\frac{165.5 + 149.33}{2} = 157.415\text{ cm}$$
  • Why it Fails: It treats both groups as equal in size!
✓
Method 2: Shreyas' Total Sum Method
  • Sum of Senior heights: $165.5 \times 8 = 1324\text{ cm}$
  • Sum of Junior heights: $149.33 \times 3 = 448\text{ cm}$
  • Combined group size: $8 + 3 = 11$ players
  • True Combined Average:
    $$\frac{1324 + 448}{11} = \frac{1772}{11} \approx 161.09\text{ cm}$$

Algebraic Formulation: Combining Two Collections

Deriving the fundamental weighted formula from first principles

1
Collection 1 & Collection 2
  • Collection 1: $n$ values $x_i$ with mean $a$: $\sum x_i = a \cdot n$
  • Collection 2: $m$ values $y_j$ with mean $b$: $\sum y_j = b \cdot m$
2
The Combined Mean Formula
Combined Average of Two Collections:
$$\text{Average} = \frac{a \cdot n + b \cdot m}{n + m}$$
  • The sizes $n$ and $m$ act as natural weights.
  • If $n > m$, the combined average is pulled closer to $a$.

When Simple Averages Work: Jaspreet's Cycling

Equal group sizes allow direct averaging of subgroup averages

Jaspreet's 3-Week Cycling Log
  • Week 1 (5 days): Mean $a = 12.8\text{ min}$
  • Week 2 (5 days): Mean $b = 15.8\text{ min}$
  • Week 3 (5 days): Mean $c = 18.0\text{ min}$
  • Every week has exactly $p = 5$ days!
The Algebraic Reason for Equality
$$\frac{ap + bp + cp}{p + p + p} = \frac{a+b+c}{3}$$
  • $\frac{12.8 + 15.8 + 18.0}{3} \approx 15.53\text{ minutes}$
  • Universal Rule: When all collections are of equal size, the average of their averages equals the true overall mean.

Mixtures: Equal vs Unequal Concentrations

How volume proportions dictate final concentration

Example 3: Equal Volumes
  • Two glasses of lemonade: Glass 1 has 10% jaggery, Glass 2 has 20%.
  • Since volumes are identical, the result is exactly midway ($15\%$).
Example 4: Unequal Volumes
  • Bowl: $500\text{ mL}$ with 10% jaggery $\implies 50\text{ mL}$
  • Glass: $200\text{ mL}$ with 20% jaggery $\implies 40\text{ mL}$
  • Final Conc.: $\frac{50 + 40}{500 + 200} = \frac{90}{700} \approx 13\%$
  • The mixture sits closer to 10% than 20%.

Combining Three Batches: The Brass Alloy Problem

Solving for an unknown batch weight using weighted mean principles

Given Batches of Brass (Copper + Zinc)
  • Batch A: $200\text{ kg}$, $70\%$ Copper $\implies 140\text{ kg}$
  • Batch B: $120\text{ kg}$, $50\%$ Copper $\implies 60\text{ kg}$
  • Batch C: $y\text{ kg}$, $45\%$ Copper
  • Target Mixture: $55\%$ Copper overall
Algebraic Solution for Batch C
$$0.55 = \frac{200(0.70) + 120(0.50) + y(0.45)}{200 + 120 + y}$$
  • $0.55(320 + y) = 200 + 0.45y$
  • $0.10y = 24 \implies \mathbf{y = 240\text{ kg}}$

The General Weighted Mean Definition

Weighting values by magnitude, volume, frequency, or importance

Definition: Weighted Mean ($\overline{x}$)

For $n$ quantities $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ associated with corresponding weights $w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n$:

$$\overline{x} = \frac{w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + \dots + w_n x_n}{w_1 + w_2 + \dots + w_n} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i}{\sum_{i=1}^n w_i}$$
What is a "Weight"?

Weights can be physical (e.g., volume in mL, mass in kg) or abstract (e.g., credit hours, priority scale).

Key Property

The weighted average always satisfies:

$$\min(x_i) \le \overline{x} \le \max(x_i)$$

Stacked Bar Charts: Totals with Component Breakdown

Combining sub-segments into a single cumulative bar

How Stacked Bars Work
  • Bars are placed end-to-end to form one composite bar.
  • The total length represents the grand total.
  • Visual representation of Table 10.2:
    1940
    1700
    1280

Advantages & Trade-Offs
  • Huge Advantage: Instantly compares totals across groups at a single glance.
  • The Floating Baseline Trade-Off: Only the first category starts from zero!
  • All middle segments float without a common baseline, making subtle differences between interior components hard to judge accurately.

100% Stacked Bars: A Linear Alternative to Pie Charts

Comparing relative proportions rather than absolute totals

Why Replace Pie Charts?
  • Pie Charts: Human eyes struggle to compare angles tilted at different orientations.
  • 100% Stacked Bar: Normalizes all bars to a fixed $100\%$ uniform width.
  • Segments directly reflect the percentage share contributed by each category.
Powering Up: Electricity Shift (2005 vs 2025)
  • 2005 (Total 1000 kWh): Lighting: $36\%$, Cooling: $30\%$, Kitchen: $12\%$, Other: $22\%$
  • 2025 (Total 2000 kWh): Lighting: $11\%$, Cooling: $40\%$, Kitchen: $23\%$, Other: $26\%$
  • Crucial Takeaway: Lighting share dropped dramatically from $36\%$ to $11\%$ due to efficient LEDs, while Cooling surged to $40\%$!

What Can We Infer? The Garden Flower Paradox

A higher percentage share does NOT guarantee a higher absolute count

The 100% Stacked Chart Data
  • Fatima's Blooms: Summer $50\%$, Monsoon $30\%$, Winter $20\%$
  • Naveen's Blooms: Summer $40\%$, Monsoon $25\%$, Winter $35\%$
  • Valid Inference (Within Same Garden): Fatima definitely had more summer blooms than monsoon blooms ($50\% > 30\%$).
The Fallacy Across Gardens
  • "Did Fatima have more summer blooms than Naveen?" UNKNOWN!
  • Case 1: Fatima total $= 210$, Naveen total $= 120$: Fatima Summer $= 105 >$ Naveen Summer $= 48$.
  • Case 2: Fatima total $= 180$, Naveen total $= 240$: Fatima Summer $= 90 <$ Naveen Summer $= 96$.
  • Takeaway: Proportions cannot reveal absolute winners across groups without knowing the base totals!

Exercise Highlights — Step-by-Step Solutions

Three representative problems calculated from the exercise sets

1. Test Scores (Set 10.1, Q1)

Given: Sec A: 30 students, mean $= 72$. Sec B: 25 students, mean $= 76$.

Formula: $\overline{x} = \frac{30(72) + 25(76)}{30 + 25}$

$$= \frac{2160 + 1900}{55} = \frac{4060}{55}$$

$\overline{x} \approx 73.82$
2. Panipuri Pani Dilution (Q4)

Given: $10\text{ L}$ pani has $8\%$ spice. Target: $\frac{3}{4} \times 8\% = 6\%$ spice by adding water $W$.

Spice amount $= 10 \times 0.08 = 0.8\text{ L}$.

$$\frac{0.8}{10 + W} = 0.06 \implies 0.8 = 0.6 + 0.06W$$

$W = \frac{0.2}{0.06} = 3.33\text{ Litres}$
3. Ancient Pool Depth (Q4)

Lengths $w_i$: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 (Sum $= 30$).

Depths $x_i$: 9, 7, 7, 3, 2.

$\sum w_i x_i = 36 + 35 + 42 + 21 + 16 = 150$.

$\overline{x} = \frac{150}{30} = 5\text{ hastas}$

Chapter Summary: Data Toolkit

Master recap of weighted averages, mixtures, and stacked visualizations

Mathematical Principles (✅)
  • Weighted Average Formula:
    $$\overline{x} = \frac{w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + \dots + w_n x_n}{w_1 + w_2 + \dots + w_n}$$
  • Symmetry: If all weights are equal ($w_i = k$), weighted mean reduces to the simple arithmetic mean.
  • Scale Invariance: Multiplying all weights by a constant factor $c$ leaves the weighted average unchanged.
  • Mixtures & Alloys: Quantities combine in direct proportion to their masses or volumes.
Visualisation Rules (✅)
  • Cluster Columns: Compare items directly against a common baseline at $0$.
  • Stacked Bars: Compare composite totals while preserving sub-component contributions.
  • 100% Stacked Bars: Compare internal proportions and shares across different groups; does not reveal absolute sizes.
  • Directional Conversion: Stacked Bar $\implies$ 100% Stacked Bar (Always possible); 100% Stacked Bar $\implies$ Stacked Bar (Impossible without knowing total sizes).
