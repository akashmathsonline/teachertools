1.1 Percentages Intro

🔍 What is a Percentage? Per Centum Decoded

Page 1–2
📖 Etymology & Definition

The symbol $\%$ is read as per cent, derived from the Latin phrase 'per centum', meaning 'by the hundred' or 'out of a hundred'.

$$z\% = \frac{z}{100} = z \times \frac{1}{100}$$
  • $25\%$ means $25$ out of every $100$ (marks, people, rupees).
  • $50\%$ of a quantity $s$: $50 \times \frac{1}{100} \times s = \frac{1}{2}s$ (exactly half).
  • Core Principle: Percentages are simply fractions whose denominator is fixed at $100$.

1.1 Deep Dive

⚖️ Why Choose 100? The Base-10 Advantage

Page 4
🍪 The Biscuit Challenge

A factory tests 2 biscuit recipes:

Variety 1 has $\frac{9}{34}$ sugar.
Variety 2 has $\frac{13}{45}$ sugar.
Which is sweeter? Hard to tell at a glance!

Converting to common base 100: $$26.47\% \quad \text{vs} \quad 28.88\%$$ Variety 2 is immediately recognized as more sugary!

🔟 Base-10 Decimal Fit

Because human civilization uses a base-10 positional numeral system, numbers like $10, 100, 1000$ mesh naturally with decimals:

$$31\% = \frac{31}{100} = 0.31$$

Interchanging fractions, decimals, and percentages is lightning-fast without awkward long division.

🎯 The Cognitive Sweet Spot

Why not $10$ or $1000$?

  • Per 10 (per decem): Too coarse ($\frac{9}{34} = 2.647$ per 10).
  • Per 1000 (per mille): Too granular for mental grasp ($\frac{9}{34} = 264.7‰$).
  • Per 100: Large enough to give meaningful resolution, yet small enough to visualize mentally.

1.1 Real World & History

📜 Ancient Roots & Percentages Around Us

Page 5
🏛️ 2,400 Years of "Per Cent"

Long before modern decimals, ancient civilizations required proportional rates:

  • Arthashastra (4th c. BCE, India): Kautilya documented monthly interest per cent: "An interest of a pana and a quarter per month per cent is just. Five panas per cent is commercial interest..."
  • Ancient Rome: Auction & trade taxes levied as $\frac{1}{20}$ and $\frac{1}{100}$.
  • 15th Century Italy: Merchants abbreviated percentages as 'xx p cento' ($20\%$) and 'x p cento' ($10\%$).
🌍 Percentages Around Us Today
  • Human Body: $\approx 60\%$ water by total body weight.
  • Ice Cream: $\approx 30\% - 50\%$ air by volume!
  • 2022 FIFA World Cup: $45\%$ of global population watched.
  • Solar System Mass: Sun contains $99.86\%$; Jupiter has $0.071\%$; Earth is just $0.0003\%$.
  • Global Agriculture: An estimated $52\%$ of arable land is degraded.

1.2 Proportions

🍪 Percentage of a Quantity: Respect the Whole!

Page 6–7
⚠️ Percentages Alone Do Not Equal Amounts!

Madhu ate biscuits with $25\%$ sugar. Madhav ate biscuits with $35\%$ sugar. Who consumed more sugar?

  • If wholes are identical ($100\text{ g}$): Madhav ate more ($35\text{ g} > 25\text{ g}$).
  • If Madhu ate $120\text{ g}$ and Madhav ate $95\text{ g}$:
  • Madhu: $25\%$ of $120\text{ g} = \frac{25}{100} \times 120 = \mathbf{30\text{ g sugar}}$
  • Madhav: $35\%$ of $95\text{ g} = \frac{35}{100} \times 95 = \mathbf{33.25\text{ g sugar}}$
$$\text{Amount} = \frac{y}{100} \times \text{Total Quantity } (Q)$$
🔍 Proportional Reasoning Models

Connecting unitary method, fractions, and proportions:

Unitary Method:
$100\text{ g}$ biscuits $\to 35\text{ g}$ sugar
$1\text{ g}$ biscuit $\to \frac{35}{100}\text{ g}$ sugar
$95\text{ g}$ biscuits $\to \frac{35}{100} \times 95 = 33.25\text{ g}$

Proportion equation: $\frac{35}{100} = \frac{s}{95} \implies s = 33.25\text{ g}$

1.2 Mental Math

🧠 Free-Hand Mental Calculations & Building Blocks

Page 7–8
🧩 Benchmark Percentages
  • $50\%$: Divide by $2$ (half).
  • $25\%$: Half of $50\%$ (quarter, divide by $4$).
  • $10\%$: Shift decimal left by $1$ place (divide by $10$).
  • $5\%$: Half of $10\%$.
  • $1\%$: Shift decimal left by $2$ places (divide by $100$).
$$(20\% \text{ of } y) + (5\% \text{ of } y) = 25\% \text{ of } y$$
⚡ Decomposing Any Percentage

Combine simple building blocks mentally without paper:

  • $15\%$ of $80$: $(10\% \text{ of } 80) + (5\% \text{ of } 80) = 8 + 4 = \mathbf{12}$.
  • $75\%$ of $80$: $50\% + 25\% = 40 + 20 = \mathbf{60}$.
  • $40\%$ of $80$: $4 \times (10\%) = 4 \times 8 = \mathbf{32}$.
  • $90\%$ of $80$: $100\% - 10\% = 80 - 8 = \mathbf{72}$.

🔄 Fractions, Decimals, Percentages & Ratios

🔁 The Three Interchangeable Faces

Every fraction has an exact decimal and percentage identity:

Fraction Decimal Percentage
$1/2$$0.5$$50\%$

$1/5$$0.2$$20\%$

🥣 Converting Ratios to %: Millet Kanji

Millet to water ratio is $2:7$. Total parts $= 2 + 7 = 9$.

  • $\text{Millet share} = \frac{2}{9}$ of the mixture.
  • $\text{Millet \%} = \frac{2}{9} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{22.22\%}$.
  • $\text{Water \%} = \frac{7}{9} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{77.78\%}$.
  • In $500\text{ ml}$ kanji: $22.22\% \text{ of } 500 = \mathbf{111.1\text{ ml millet}}$.

1.2 Beyond 100%

📈 Can Percentages Exceed 100%?

Page 10–12
🎯 Kishanlal's Daily Sales Targets

Kishanlal targets daily sales of ₹$5000$ ($= 100\%$ target).

  • Day 1 (₹2000): $\frac{2000}{5000} \times 100 = 40\%$ ($60\%$ short of goal).
  • Day 3 (₹5000): $\frac{5000}{5000} \times 100 = \mathbf{100\%}$ (exact goal achieved).
  • Day 4 (₹6000): ₹$1000$ over goal $\implies \frac{6000}{5000} \times 100 = \mathbf{120\%}$ ($20\%$ surplus!).
  • Day 5 (₹7800): $\frac{7800}{5000} \times 100 = \mathbf{156\%}$.
🌾 Harvest Multipliers

Example 7: Last year's wheat harvest was $260\text{ kg}$. This year's harvest is $650\text{ kg}$.

$$\frac{650}{260} \times 100 = 250\%$$

$250\%$ indicates the new harvest is exactly $2.5$ times the baseline reference harvest ($650 = 2.5 \times 260$).

1.3 Rate of Change

📊 Percentage Increase and Decrease

Page 15–16
🍅 Price Increase (Inflation)

Tomato price rose from ₹$30$ to ₹$42$ per kg over 3 years:

$$\% \text{ Increase} = \frac{\text{Increase Amount}}{\text{Original Base}} \times 100$$

$$\frac{42 - 30}{30} \times 100 = \frac{12}{30} \times 100 = \mathbf{40\%}$$ Key Rule: The denominator is always the original baseline, never the new value!

🎭 Footfall Decrease (Drop)

Theater footfall fell from $160$ patrons to $100$ patrons:

$$\% \text{ Decrease} = \frac{\text{Decrease Amount}}{\text{Original Base}} \times 100$$

$$\frac{160 - 100}{160} \times 100 = \frac{60}{160} \times 100 = \mathbf{37.5\%}$$ Statement equivalence: "Population in 1991 is $165\%$ of 1961" $\equiv$ "Population increased by $65\%$".

Commercial Math

💼 The Supply Chain: Cost, Marked & Selling Price

Page 16–18
🧶 Sweater Supply Chain Journey
  • Manufacturer: $\text{CP} = ₹230, \ \text{MP} = ₹255, \ \text{SP} = ₹253$.
  • Wholesaler: $\text{CP} = ₹253, \ \text{MP} = ₹310, \ \text{SP} = ₹300$.
  • Retailer (Kishanlal): $\text{CP} = ₹300, \ \text{MP} = ₹480, \ \text{SP} = ₹430$.
$$\text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} \quad (\text{if SP} > \text{CP})$$ $$\text{Profit } \% = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$$

Kishanlal's profit: $₹430 - ₹300 = ₹130 \implies \frac{130}{300} \times 100 = \mathbf{43.33\%}$.

🏢 Gross Profit vs Net Profit
  • Gross Profit: Sales Revenue minus Cost of goods sold ($\text{Revenue} - \text{Procurement}$).
  • Net Profit: Gross Profit minus operating overheads (rent, salaries, electricity, transport).
  • Loss: Occurs when $\text{SP} < \text{CP}$.
    $\text{Loss } \% = \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$.

Commercial Math

🧾 Understanding Taxes: GST in India

Page 19
🏛️ Goods and Services Tax (GST)

GST is an indirect value-added consumption tax levied on goods and services, divided equally between central and state governments:

  • CGST (Central GST): Goes to the Union Government.
  • SGST (State GST): Goes to the State Government.
  • Total tax $= \text{CGST rate} + \text{SGST rate}$.
  • Added directly to the bill total paid by the end customer.
💡 Deconstructing a Tax Invoice
XY Electricals Receipt:
3 CFL Bulbs @ ₹150 each $\implies \text{Sub Total} = \mathbf{₹450.00}$
CGST @ 9%: $0.09 \times 450 = \mathbf{₹40.50}$
SGST @ 9%: $0.09 \times 450 = \mathbf{₹40.50}$
$$\text{Total Invoice} = 450 + 40.50 + 40.50 = \mathbf{₹531.00}$$

Multiplier trick: $450 \times 1.18 = \mathbf{₹531.00}$ (18% total GST).

Growth & Banking

📈 Growth & Compounding: The Power of Reinvestment

Page 20–24
Option 1: Linear Simple Growth

Interest is withdrawn every year. Principal remains constant ($P$):

$$A = P(1 + rt)$$

₹$6000$ at $10\%$ for 3 years:
Earns $₹600 + ₹600 + ₹600 = ₹1800$
$\text{Total} = 6000 + 1800 = \mathbf{₹7800}$ ($30\%$ gain).

Option 2: Compounded Exponential Growth

Earned interest is added back to principal each cycle:

$$A = P(1 + r)^t$$

₹$6000$ compounded at $10\%$ for 3 years:
$6000 \times 1.1 \times 1.1 \times 1.1 = \mathbf{₹7986}$ ($33.1\%$ gain).
Bonus from Compounding: An extra ₹$186$ earned on interest!

Tricky Percentages

🎂 Successive Discounts & The Pricing Fallacy

Page 26–27
🍰 Cakely (30% + 20%) vs Cakify (50%)

Does $30\% + 20\%$ off equal $50\%$ off? No!

  • Consider a ₹$200$ cake:
  • Cakify ($50\%$ flat): Price $= ₹200 - 100 = \mathbf{₹100}$.
  • Cakely ($30\% + 20\%$ successive):
    After $30\% \implies ₹200 - 60 = ₹140$.
    After $20\%$ on ₹$140 \implies ₹140 - 28 = \mathbf{₹112}$.
  • Cakify is cheaper by ₹$12$! Successive discounts apply to a reduced base.
⚡ Surbhi's 50% Margin & 50% Discount Mishap

Surbhi marks goods up by $50\%$, then gives a $50\%$ discount:

$$x \xrightarrow{+50\%} 1.5x \xrightarrow{-50\%} 1.5x \times 0.5 = 0.75x$$

She suffers a net $25\%$ loss! To return to baseline cost from $1.5x$, the required discount is $\frac{0.5x}{1.5x} = \mathbf{33.33\%}$, not $50\%$.

Exercise Highlights

⭐ Key Worked Exemplars: Exercises

Page 28–29, 32
1. Left-Handed Room Puzzle

In a room of $100$ people, $99$ are left-handed ($99\%$). How many left-handers must leave so that left-handers become $98\%$?

Right-handers remain exactly $1$ person.
For $1$ to represent $2\%$ of total ($T$):
$$0.02 \times T = 1 \implies T = 50$$ Left-handers needed $= 50 - 1 = 49$.
Must leave: $99 - 49 = \mathbf{50\text{ people!}}$
2. Symmetry: x% of y = y% of x

Calculate $16\%$ of $250$. Use algebraic symmetry!

$$x\% \text{ of } y = \frac{x \cdot y}{100} = \frac{y \cdot x}{100} = y\% \text{ of } x$$ $$16\% \text{ of } 250 = 250\% \text{ of } 16$$ $$= 2.5 \times 16 = \mathbf{40}$$ Mentally far easier than computing $16 \times 2.5$!
3. Buy 2 Get 1 Free Deal

What is the true percentage discount in a "Buy 2 Get 1 Free" offer?

Total items received $= 2 + 1 = 3$.
Free items $= 1$.
$$\text{Discount } \% = \frac{\text{Free}}{\text{Total}} \times 100$$ $$= \frac{1}{3} \times 100 = \mathbf{33.33\%}$$ (Not $50\%$ as commonly mistaken!)

Chapter Summary

📋 Master Summary Checklist

Page 31
✅ Percentage Foundations
  • Definition: Fractions with denominator $100$ ($z\% = \frac{z}{100}$).
  • The FDP Trio: Effortless interconversion between Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages ($\frac{4}{10} = 0.4 = 40\%$).
  • Additive Property: Break awkward percentages into easy mental chunks ($20\% + 5\% = 25\%$).
  • Symmetry Law: $x\% \text{ of } y = y\% \text{ of } x = \frac{xy}{100}$.
✅ Applied Commercial & Growth Rules
  • Rate of Change: $\frac{\text{Change}}{\text{Original Base}} \times 100$ (base is always the starting value).
  • Commercial Math: $\text{Profit } \% = \frac{\text{SP} - \text{CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$; $\text{Loss } \% = \frac{\text{CP} - \text{SP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$.
  • GST Structure: $\text{Total GST} = \text{CGST} + \text{SGST}$.
  • Compounding Formula: $A = P(1 + r)^t$ vs simple linear growth $A = P(1 + rt)$.
  • Depreciation: Value after $t$ periods $= P(1 - r)^t$.
