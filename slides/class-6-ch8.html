✏️ What is a Curve in Geometry?
  • Broad Definition: Any continuous line or shape drawn smoothly on paper with a pencil without lifting it.
  • Includes straight line segments, circular loops, wavy contours, and irregular closed shapes.
  • Freehand drawing often lacks uniformity; compasses and rulers transform freeform art into rigorous geometry.
📐 The Compass Tool Exploration
  • Structure: One arm has a metal needle tip (fixed pivot); the other holds a firmly clamped pencil.
  • Fixed Center: Keeps the pivot point $P$ unmoved while sweeping the pencil along the plane.
  • Constant Distance: Maintains an unvarying distance between needle point and pencil tip.

⭕ The Circle: Locus of Equidistant Points

Formally defining center, radius, and boundary distance

⭐ Formal Geometric Properties
  • The Center ($P$): The unique fixed reference point about which all boundary points revolve.
  • The Radius ($r$): The constant, fixed distance between the center $P$ and any point on the boundary circle ($r = PQ = 4\text{ cm}$).
  • Boundary Invariance: Every point $X$ on the circle satisfies the equality:
    $$\text{Distance}(P, X) = r = \text{Constant}$$
  • If a point lies inside, $\text{dist} < r$; if outside, $\text{dist} > r$.

🔲 Fundamental Properties: Rectangles & Squares

Strict mathematical definitions distinguishing opposite and adjacent properties

▭ Properties of a Rectangle
  • R1 (Opposite Sides Equal): Side $AB = CD$ and Side $AD = BC$.
  • R2 (All Right Angles): Every interior angle is strictly a right angle:
    $$\angle A = \angle B = \angle C = \angle D = 90^\circ$$
  • Opposite sides never meet and maintain a constant separation distance.
⏹️ Properties of a Square
  • S1 (All Four Sides Equal): Every boundary segment is congruent:
    $$AB = BC = CD = DA$$
  • S2 (All Right Angles): $\angle A = \angle B = \angle C = \angle D = 90^\circ$.
  • Core Insight: Every square is a special rectangle where adjacent sides happen to be equal!

🔄 Rotated Squares: Invariance Under Rotation

Why orientation changes on a dot grid do not alter geometric classification

💎 The "Diamond" Fallacy
  • When a square is rotated by $45^\circ$, children often mistakenly call it a "diamond" or assume it ceased to be a square.
  • Rigid Motion Principle: Rotating a 2D shape does not alter any side length or interior angle.
  • Since all sides remain equal and all four corner angles remain $90^\circ$, it remains 100% a square.
🎯 Dot Grid Corner Analysis
  • Slope & Right Angles: On an isometric or square dot paper, a tilted segment moving 2 right, 1 up is perpendicular to 1 right, 2 down.
  • Equal step vectors ($\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2$) guarantee equal sides.
  • You can prove whether a tilted shape is a square purely from dot coordinates without using a ruler!

📐 Constructing a Square of Side 6 cm

Step-by-step procedure using ruler, protractor, and compass

1️⃣ Base & Right Angle
1
Draw segment $PQ = 6\text{ cm}$ using a ruler.
2
At point $P$, erect a perpendicular ray ($90^\circ$) using a protractor.
2️⃣ Marking Vertical Arms
3
Open compass to $6\text{ cm}$. With center $P$, cut the ray at point $S$ ($PS = 6\text{ cm}$).
4
At $Q$, construct a perpendicular ray ($90^\circ$) pointing upwards.
3️⃣ Completing Vertex R
5
With compass radius $6\text{ cm}$, center $Q$, cut the ray at $R$ ($QR = 6\text{ cm}$).
6
Join points $S$ and $R$. Measure $SR = 6\text{ cm}$ and $\angle S = \angle R = 90^\circ$.

🔍 Dynamic Exploration: Moving Points on Opposing Sides

Analyzing distance variations between point $X$ on $AD$ and point $Y$ on $BC$

📏 Shortest & Longest Distances
  • Let rectangle $ABCD$ have sides $AB = 7\text{ cm}$ and $BC = 4\text{ cm}$.
  • Minimum Separation: When $X$ and $Y$ are directly opposite ($\text{dist}(A,X) = \text{dist}(B,Y)$):
    $$\text{Length}(XY) = AB = 7\text{ cm} \quad (\text{forms rectangle } ABYX)$$
  • Maximum Separation: When $X$ and $Y$ reach opposite corner extremes ($X=D, Y=B$):
    $$\text{Length}(XY) = \text{Diagonal } DB \approx 8.06\text{ cm}$$
📊 Observation Log Table
  • If $X$ moves $5\text{ mm}$ from $A$, and $Y$ moves $3\text{ cm}$ from $B$: slanted line $XY = 7.4\text{ cm}$.
  • If $X$ moves $1\text{ cm}$ from $A$, and $Y$ moves $1\text{ cm}$ from $B$: parallel line $XY = 7.0\text{ cm}$.
  • Fundamental Theorem: The perpendicular distance between two parallel lines is the shortest possible path connecting them.

🧩 Breaking Rectangles into Identical Squares

Reverse engineering side-ratio constraints for exact modular decomposition

🪟 Division into 2 Identical Squares
  • Let rectangle $ACDF$ be split by middle line $BE$ into squares $ABEF$ and $BCDE$.
  • Since $AB = AF$ and $BC = AF$, the total length must be:
    $$AC = AB + BC = 2 \times AF$$
  • Criterion: The rectangle length must be exactly twice its breadth ($\text{Ratio } 2:1$).
  • Example: If $AF = 4\text{ cm}$, then $AC = 8\text{ cm}$.
🧱 Generalization to $n$ Squares
  • Three Squares ($n = 3$): Length must equal $3 \times \text{breadth}$ ($\text{Ratio } 3:1$). Example: $9\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$.
  • Non-decomposable Examples:
    • A rectangle of $4\text{ cm} \times 2.5\text{ cm}$ cannot be split into 2 squares ($4 \ne 2 \times 2.5$).
    • A rectangle of $7\text{ cm} \times 2\text{ cm}$ cannot be split into 3 squares ($7 \ne 3 \times 2$).

🎯 Nested Shapes: Concentric Holes & Staircases

Constructing squares with central holes, falling squares, and curved bulges

⭕ Square with a Hole
  • Locating Center: Draw diagonals $PR$ and $QS$ of the square.
  • Their intersection point $O$ marks the exact geometric center.
  • Place compass needle at $O$ to draw the concentric circular hole.
🪜 Falling Squares
  • Series of consecutive squares placed corner-to-corner ($4\text{ cm}$, $4\text{ cm}$, $4\text{ cm}$).
  • Or descending sequence: $7\text{ cm} \to 5\text{ cm} \to 3\text{ cm}$.
  • Requires precise $90^\circ$ baseline steps so vertical edges remain aligned.
🌸 Square with Curves
  • A square of $8\text{ cm}$ side with outwardly bulging arcs on all four sides.
  • Compass tip is placed on perpendicular bisectors or opposite midpoints.

✖️ Diagonals of Rectangles & Squares

Investigating length equality and corner angle split behaviors

📏 Diagonal Length Equality
  • In any rectangle $PQRS$, connect opposite vertices $PR$ and $QS$.
  • Equality Rule: The two diagonals are always equal in length:
    $$\text{Diagonal } PR = \text{Diagonal } QS$$
  • They bisect each other at the geometric midpoint.
📐 Corner Angle Split: Rectangle vs. Square
  • General Rectangle: A diagonal divides $90^\circ$ into unequal complementary angles ($c \ne d$ and $g \ne h$):
    $$c + d = 90^\circ \quad (\text{e.g., } 60^\circ \text{ and } 30^\circ)$$
  • Special Square Case: Because adjacent sides are equal, the diagonal bisects the corner angles:
    $$c = d = 45^\circ \quad \text{and} \quad g = h = 45^\circ$$

📐 Constructing a Rectangle Given One Side & Diagonal

Constructing rectangle $ABCD$ where base $CD = 5\text{ cm}$ and diagonal $DB = 7\text{ cm}$

1️⃣ Base & Normal Ray
  • Draw base line segment $CD = 5\text{ cm}$.
  • At point $C$, erect a perpendicular ray line $l$ ($\angle C = 90^\circ$).
  • Point $B$ must lie somewhere along this vertical ray $l$.
2️⃣ Compass Arc for Vertex B
  • Open compass to diagonal length $7\text{ cm}$.
  • Set needle at $D$ and strike an arc intersecting ray $l$ at point $B$.
  • No trial and error needed: the arc intersection point is uniquely $B$!
3️⃣ Completing Vertex A
  • Erect perpendiculars at $D$ and $B$, or use opposite side compass arcs ($5\text{ cm}$ from $B$, $BC$ from $D$).
  • Their intersection establishes vertex $A$. Join $AB$ and $AD$.

🏠 Points Equidistant from Two Points: The "House"

Locating a point $A$ at equal distance from two given base points $B$ and $C$

🏠 Roof Peak Construction (Point A)
  • Given two wall tops $B$ and $C$ ($BC = 5\text{ cm}$). We need roof vertex $A$ such that $AB = 5\text{ cm}$ and $AC = 5\text{ cm}$.
  • With center $B$ and radius $5\text{ cm}$, draw an arc above $BC$.
  • With center $C$ and radius $5\text{ cm}$, draw another arc intersecting the first arc at $A$.
  • Join $AB$ and $AC$. Triangle $\triangle ABC$ is an equilateral triangle!
🏹 The Curved Ceiling Arc
  • To draw the ceiling arc curving downward from $B$ to $C$:
  • Place compass needle at apex $A$.
  • With radius $r = AB = AC = 5\text{ cm}$, sweep the arc between $B$ and $C$.
  • Rhombal Geometry: The same intersecting arc technique constructs a rhombus (all sides equal, angles not $90^\circ$).

📝 Exercise Highlights: Step-by-Step Solutions

Representative problems worked out from textbook exercises

1️⃣ Semicircular Wave Radius

Q: Given baseline $AB = 8\text{ cm}$ with two identical waves. Find length of $AX$ and radius.

• Baseline is halved: $AX = 8 \div 2 = 4\text{ cm}$.
• Semicircle diameter is $AX = 4\text{ cm}$.
• Compass radius $r = 4 \div 2 = 2\text{ cm}$.
$$r = 2\text{ cm}, \quad AX = 4\text{ cm}$$
2️⃣ Diagonal $45^\circ$ Split

Q: A diagonal divides a rectangle's corner into $45^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. What shape is it?

• In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle B = 90^\circ$, $\angle CAB = 45^\circ$.
• $\angle BCA = 180^\circ - (90^\circ + 45^\circ) = 45^\circ$.
• Isosceles triangle: $AB = BC$.
$$\text{Sides equal } \implies \text{It is a Square!}$$
3️⃣ Rectangle (Side 4, Diag 8)

Q: Construct rectangle $PQRS$ with side $4\text{ cm}$ and diagonal $8\text{ cm}$.

• Draw base $PQ = 4\text{ cm}$, ray at $P \perp PQ$.
• Arc from $Q$ with radius $8\text{ cm}$ cuts ray at $S$.
• Height $PS = \sqrt{8^2 - 4^2} \approx 6.93\text{ cm}$.
$$\text{Height } PS \approx 6.93\text{ cm}$$

📋 Comprehensive Chapter Synthesis

All fundamental theorems and construction techniques in one checklist

✅ Circle & Quadrilateral Rules
  • Circle Locus: All points on a circle lie at the exact same radial distance $r$ from the fixed center point $P$.
  • Rectangle: Opposite sides equal ($AB = CD$, $AD = BC$) and all four angles equal $90^\circ$.
  • Square: All four sides equal and all four angles $90^\circ$. A rotated square remains a square.
  • Modular Partition: A rectangle divides into $n$ identical squares if and only if $\text{length} = n \times \text{breadth}$.
✅ Diagonals & Compass Intersections
  • Diagonal Equality: Diagonals of a rectangle are always equal in length ($PR = QS$).
  • Angle Bisector Property: Diagonals bisect corner angles ($45^\circ, 45^\circ$) if and only if the rectangle is a square.
  • Arc Intersection Method: Locates a point at distance $d$ along a perpendicular ray without ruler trial and error.
  • Equidistant Points: Intersecting two arcs of identical radius locates vertices equidistant from two reference points.
