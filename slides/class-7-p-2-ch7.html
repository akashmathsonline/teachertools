Unknown Weights: The Balance Principle

A physical balance scale serves as our fundamental geometric and algebraic model: **both plates must hold identical total weight to remain horizontal and balanced**[cite: 29].
⚖️ The Concept of Balance

When equal weights sit on both sides, the scale is perfectly horizontal[cite: 29]. An unknown weight can be deduced directly if its counterpart is known[cite: 29].

$\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$
🥪 Slices & Fried Eggs

Fig. 7.6 shows 3 slices of bread (weight 2 each) balancing 2 fried eggs[cite: 30, 33]:

$2 + 2 + 2 = e + e \implies 2e = 6$[cite: 33]

Each egg $e$ has an unknown weight of 3 units[cite: 30, 33].

🌸 Mobiles & Hanging Rods

In hanging decorative mobiles (Figs. 7.1–7.5), a master rod splits total weight evenly across both branches[cite: 29, 30].

$\text{Branch Weight} = \frac{\text{Total}}{2}$

The Principle of Removing Equal Quantities

⚖️ Sacks on Both Plates (Fig. 7.10)

Consider 2 sacks on the left plate balancing 1 sack + a 10 kg block + a 4 kg weight on the right[cite: 30]:

$2s = s + 14$[cite: 30]
  • Physical Strategy: Remove exactly 1 sack from both plates simultaneously[cite: 30].
  • The scale remains completely horizontal[cite: 30, 34].
  • Leftover on left: 1 sack; leftover on right: 14 kg. Hence, $s = 14\text{ kg}$[cite: 30]!
📦 Large Sacks Problem (Fig. 7.12)

Plate A has 90 sacks + a 50 kg block[cite: 31]. Plate B has 60 sacks + a 500 kg container[cite: 31].

$90s + 50 = 60s + 500$[cite: 31]
  • Step 1: Remove 60 sacks from both plates $\implies 30s + 50 = 500$[cite: 31].
  • Step 2: Remove 50 kg from both plates $\implies 30s = 450$[cite: 31].
  • Step 3: Divide equally $\implies s = 15\text{ kg}$ per sack[cite: 31]!
Fundamental Rule of Algebra: Removing equal quantities from both sides leaves all unknowns grouped on one plate, isolating the value systematically[cite: 31, 34].

Matchstick Patterns: From Sequence to Equation

🔥 Growing Triangles

Consider the sequence of joined matchstick triangles[cite: 31]:

  • Position 1: $2(1) + 1 = 3$ sticks[cite: 32]
  • Position 2: $2(2) + 1 = 5$ sticks[cite: 32]
  • Position 3: $2(3) + 1 = 7$ sticks[cite: 32]
  • Position 4: $2(4) + 1 = 9$ sticks[cite: 31, 32]
$n^{\text{th}}\text{ pattern} = 2n + 1$[cite: 32]
🧩 Jasmine's Challenge

Jasmine decides to build an arrangement in this sequence using exactly 99 matchsticks[cite: 32]. What position number $n$ will it be[cite: 32]?

$\underbrace{2n + 1}_{\text{LHS}} = \underbrace{99}_{\text{RHS}}$[cite: 32]

What is an Equation? A statement of equality between two algebraic expressions[cite: 32]. The letter-number (variable) $n$ represents the unknown position to be discovered[cite: 32].

Can we have an arrangement with 200 sticks[cite: 32]? Since $2n + 1 = 200 \implies 2n = 199 \implies n = 99.5$, which is not a whole number, no such pattern exists[cite: 32]!

The Trial and Error Method & Its Limitations

🔍 Testing Consecutive Values for $2n + 1 = 99$

We substitute sample test values into the LHS to see when it reaches 99[cite: 33]:

  • Try $n = 5 \implies \text{LHS} = 2(5) + 1 = 11$ (far too low)[cite: 33]
  • Try $n = 10 \implies \text{LHS} = 21$ (still too low)[cite: 33]
  • Try $n = 30 \implies \text{LHS} = 61$ (closer)[cite: 33]
  • Try $n = 40 \implies \text{LHS} = 81$[cite: 33]
  • Try $n = 50 \implies \text{LHS} = 101$ (slightly too high!)[cite: 33]
  • Try $n = 49 \implies \text{LHS} = 2(49) + 1 = 99 = \text{RHS}$ ✅[cite: 33]
⚠️ Why Trial & Error Is Inefficient

Consider solving an equation like[cite: 33]:

$5x - 4 = 7$[cite: 33]
  • If $x = 2 \implies 5(2) - 4 = 6$ (too small).
  • If $x = 3 \implies 5(3) - 4 = 11$ (too large!).
  • The actual solution is a fractional value: $x = \frac{11}{5} = 2.2$[cite: 35].
  • Verdict: Guessing non-integer values is tedious and slow[cite: 33]. We need a guaranteed, systematic algebraic method[cite: 34]!

The Principle of Inverse Operations

Axiom of Equality: If the same operation (addition, subtraction, multiplication, or non-zero division) is performed on both sides of an equation, equality is preserved[cite: 34, 56].
🔄 Inverse Arithmetic Operations
  • Addition & Subtraction: Mutually undo each other[cite: 34]. To eliminate $+88$, subtract $88$ from both sides[cite: 34].
  • Multiplication & Division: Mutually undo each other[cite: 34]. To eliminate a factor of $7$, divide both sides by $7$[cite: 34].
  • Eliminating Negatives: To eliminate $-(-67)$, add $(-67)$ to both sides[cite: 35].
🎯 Systematic Example: $5x - 4 = 7$
$5x - 4 + 4 = 7 + 4 \implies 5x = 11$[cite: 35]
$\frac{5x}{5} = \frac{11}{5} \implies x = \frac{11}{5}$[cite: 35]

Verification: $\text{LHS} = 5\left(\frac{11}{5}\right) - 4 = 11 - 4 = 7 = \text{RHS}$ ✅[cite: 36]

Transposition & Unknowns on Both Sides

⏩ The Rules of Transposition
  • Additive Inverse: A term moved across the equals sign changes its sign: $+a$ becomes $-a$, and $-b$ becomes $+b$[cite: 38].
    $2y + 7 = 21 \implies 2y = 21 - 7 = 14$[cite: 36, 37, 38]
  • Multiplicative Inverse: A multiplying factor on one side becomes a divisor on the other side[cite: 38]:
    $2y = 14 \implies y = \frac{14}{2} = 7$[cite: 37, 38]
  • Divisor Transposition: A dividing term becomes a multiplier on the other side: $\frac{u}{15} = 6 \implies u = 6 \times 15 = 90$[cite: 37, 38].
👥 Variables on Both Sides: $6y + 7 = 4y + 21$

When variables appear on both sides, subtract the smaller variable term from both sides first[cite: 36]:

$6y - 4y + 7 = 21 \implies 2y + 7 = 21$[cite: 36, 38]
$2y = 21 - 7 = 14 \implies y = 7$[cite: 37, 38]
Check: $\text{LHS} = 6(7) + 7 = 49$. $\text{RHS} = 4(7) + 21 = 49$. Balanced[cite: 36, 37]!

Algebraic Mind Games: Riyaz & Akash's Trick

🎩 Riyaz's Mind Reader Steps
  • 1 Think of any number $x$[cite: 43].
  • 2 Subtract 3: $x - 3$[cite: 43].
  • 3 Multiply by 4: $4(x - 3) = 4x - 12$[cite: 43].
  • 4 Add 8: $4x - 12 + 8 = 4x - 4$[cite: 43].
  • 5 Reveal final answer: $24$[cite: 43]!
🔓 The Secret Algebraic Model

Riyaz easily unmasks the secret number by solving the linear equation[cite: 43]:

$4x - 4 = 24 \implies 4x = 28 \implies x = 7$[cite: 43]
The Secret Formula: Notice that $4x - 4 = 4(x - 1)$[cite: 43]. Therefore: $$\text{Starting Number } x = \frac{\text{Final Answer} + 4}{4} = \frac{\text{Final Answer}}{4} + 1$$ To reverse the trick instantly in your head: Divide by 4, then add 1!

Generating Equations: Reversing the Path

Starting from a fixed solution (such as $y = 5$), we can generate endlessly diverse equations by performing balanced operations on both sides[cite: 44, 45].
⛓️ Chain A (Multiplication & Inversion)
  • Start with: $y = 5$[cite: 45]
  • Subtract 6 from both sides: $y - 6 = -1$[cite: 45]
  • Subtract $y$ from both sides: $-6 = -1 - y$[cite: 45]
  • Multiply by $-1$: $y + 1 = 6$[cite: 45]

Every single intermediate equation maintains $y = 5$ as its unique solution[cite: 45]!

⛓️ Chain B (Expansion & Regrouping)
  • Start with: $y = 5$[cite: 45]
  • Add 2 to both sides: $y + 2 = 7$[cite: 45]
  • Multiply both sides by 3: $3(y + 2) = 21 \implies 3y + 6 = 21$[cite: 45]
  • Subtract 6 from both sides: $3y = 15$[cite: 45]

Moving top-to-bottom or bottom-to-top uses exact inverse operations[cite: 45].

Mind the Mistake, Mend the Mistake

❌ Sign Error in Transposition

Erroneous Step:
$4x + 6 = 10 \implies 4x = 10 + 6 = 16$[cite: 46]

Mistake: $+6$ changed to $+6$[cite: 46]!

Correction: Transposition requires additive inverse: $4x = 10 - 6 = 4 \implies x = 1$[cite: 38, 46].

❌ Partial Distributive Division

Erroneous Step:
$2v - 4 = 6 \implies v - 4 = 6 - 2 = 4$[cite: 46]

Mistake: Subtracted 2 instead of dividing[cite: 46]!

Correction: First transpose 4: $2v = 6 + 4 = 10 \implies v = 5$.

❌ Incomplete Term Division

Erroneous Step:
$3(7y + 4) = 9 + 5y \implies 7y + 4 = \frac{9}{3} + 5y$[cite: 47]

Mistake: Only 9 divided by 3, not $5y$[cite: 47]!

Correction: Expand parentheses first: $21y + 12 = 9 + 5y \implies 16y = -3 \implies y = -\frac{3}{16}$.

A Pinch of History: The Origins of Bījagaṇita

The Seed of Mathematics: Bīja means seed[cite: 47]. Just as a mighty tree is hidden within a tiny seed, the answer to a problem is concealed inside the unknown quantity[cite: 47]!
📜 Brahmagupta (628 CE)

In Chapter 18 of Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta, he pioneered rules for adding, subtracting, and multiplying unknown quantities using letter symbols[cite: 47]. Fields Medalist David Mumford hailed him as the central creator of modern algebra[cite: 47].

🌍 Al-Khwarizmi & Europe

In ~825 CE, Al-Khwarizmi wrote Hisab al-jabr wal-muqabala ("restoring and balancing"), influenced by Indian texts[cite: 47]. The Latin translation of al-jabr gave the modern world the word Algebra[cite: 47].

🔤 Ancient Indian Notation

Unknowns were named after colours: yā for yāvat-tāvat ("as much as"), kā (kālaka/black), nī (nīlaka/blue)[cite: 47, 48]. Known constants were called rūpa (rū)[cite: 48].

Brahmagupta's Universal Linear Equation Formula

📐 The Master Formula for $Ax + B = Cx + D$

First proposed by Aryabhata (499 CE) and systematised by Brahmagupta (628 CE)[cite: 49]:

$$x = \frac{D - B}{A - C}$$[cite: 49]

Proof by Transposition:
$Ax - Cx = D - B \implies x(A - C) = D - B \implies x = \frac{D - B}{A - C}$[cite: 49]

🐎 Bhāskarāchārya's Horse Riddle (1150 CE)

Bījagaṇita: Person 1 has ₹300 and 6 horses[cite: 48]. Person 2 has 10 horses and a debt of ₹100[cite: 48]. If both are equally wealthy, find horse price $x$[cite: 48]:

$6x + 300 = 10x - 100$[cite: 48]

Using Brahmagupta's formula with $A=6, B=300, C=10, D=-100$[cite: 48, 49]: $$x = \frac{-100 - 300}{6 - 10} = \frac{-400}{-4} = ₹100\text{ per horse}$$[cite: 48]

Problem: A giraffe's height is $2.5\text{ m}$ more than half its height[cite: 53]. Find height $h$[cite: 53].

📜 Bakhshāli Manuscript (Q14)

Problem: 4 shares: $x, 2x, 6x, 24x$ sum to 132[cite: 53]. Find share 1[cite: 53].

$x + 2x + 6x + 24x = 132$[cite: 53]

Combine terms $\implies 33x = 132$
Divide by 33 $\implies x = 4$ ✅

Chapter Summary & Core Takeaways

📋 Algebraic Principles
  • ✅ Equation Definition: A statement of equality between two algebraic expressions[cite: 32, 55].
  • ✅ Balance of Equality: Applying identical operations to both sides leaves the equation fully balanced[cite: 34, 55].
  • ✅ Transposition Rules: Transposing terms replaces them with their additive or multiplicative inverses[cite: 38].
  • ✅ Root Verification: Substituting the solution back into the LHS must identically match the RHS[cite: 36, 55].
📜 Historical & Practical Roots
  • ✅ Bījagaṇita: Indian algebra treats the unknown like a seed concealing the hidden tree[cite: 47].
  • ✅ Brahmagupta's Rule: Any equation $Ax + B = Cx + D$ is solved directly by $x = \frac{D - B}{A - C}$[cite: 49].
  • ✅ Global Impact: Arabic translations (al-jabr) preserved and spread ancient algebra to the world[cite: 47].
  • ✅ Power of Algebra: Unlocks exact solutions to real-world riddles, patterns, and budgets[cite: 32, 39, 49].
