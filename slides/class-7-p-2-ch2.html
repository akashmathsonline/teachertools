Carrom Coin Model: Magnitude and Direction

Capturing distance and displacement through positive and negative integer strikes
⚪ Motion on a 1D Track
  • A carrom coin starts at origin $0$.
  • Strike 1: Moves rightward by $a$ units ($+a$).
  • Strike 2: Moves by $b$ units (rightward $+b$ or leftward $-b$).
  • Universal Formula: Final position is always: $$P = a + b$$
  • Example: Strike 1 is $+5$ (right), Strike 2 is $-7$ (left): $$P = 5 + (-7) = -2 \quad (2\text{ units to the left of } 0)$$
📏 Capturing Two Pieces of Data
  • Every integer on the number line conveys:
    1. Magnitude: The distance / number of units.
    2. Direction: Sign ($+$ for rightward, $-$ for leftward).
  • For a movement of $-4$:
    • Magnitude $= 4$
    • Direction $= \text{Leftward}$
  • If multiple strikes occur: $P = \sum (\text{movements})$.
$$\text{Final Position } P = \text{Movement } a + \text{Movement } b$$

Token Model: Zero Pairs and Additive Inverses

Using physical cancellation of green (+1) and red (-1) counters to subtract integers
🟢 Green & 🔴 Red Tokens
  • Green token: Represents $+1$.
  • Red token: Represents $-1$.
  • Zero Pair: One green $+$ one red $= (+1) + (-1) = 0$.
  • Adding or removing zero pairs does not change the net value in the bag!
✂️ Subtracting via Zero Pairs: $(+7) - (+18)$
  • Start with $7$ green tokens. We need to remove $18$ green tokens.
  • Not enough greens! Introduce 11 zero pairs ($11$ green $+ 11$ red).
  • Total tokens: $18$ green and $11$ red (net value is still $+7$).
  • Remove $18$ green tokens.
  • What remains? 11 red tokens, representing $-11$!
🔄 Foundational Identity: Subtracting an integer is identical to adding its additive inverse: $a - b = a + (-b)$.

Multiplication of Integers: Placing & Removing Tokens

Physical interpretation of positive/negative multipliers and multiplicands
🎒 The Multiplier Rule: Positive multiplier $\implies$ Place into bag. Negative multiplier $\implies$ Remove from bag.
Case 1 & 2: Positive Multiplier
  • $4 \times 2 = 8$: Place $2$ greens into empty bag $4$ times $\implies 8$ greens $= +8$.
  • $4 \times (-2) = -8$: Place $2$ reds into empty bag $4$ times $\implies 8$ reds $= -8$.
  • Positive $\times$ Negative $= -$ Negative.
Case 3 & 4: Negative Multiplier
  • $(-4) \times 2 = -8$: Remove $2$ greens $4$ times from empty bag. Put $4 \times 2 = 8$ zero pairs, remove greens $\implies 8$ reds left $= -8$.
  • $(-4) \times (-2) = +8$: Remove $2$ reds $4$ times. Put $8$ zero pairs, remove reds $\implies 8$ greens left $= \mathbf{+8}$!
$$(+) \times (+) = (+) \quad\Big|\quad (+) \times (-) = (-) \quad\Big|\quad (-) \times (+) = (-) \quad\Big|\quad (-) \times (-) = (+)$$

HISTORICAL PERSPECTIVE BRĀHMASPHUṬASIDDHĀNTA
PAGE 35

Brahmagupta's Rules: Fortunes, Debts, and Zero (628 CE)

The first explicit formulation of signed arithmetic in world mathematical history
📜 In chapter 18 of *Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta*, Brahmagupta used Dhana (fortune/positive) and Ṛṇa (debt/negative).
⚖️ The Sanskrit Axioms (Verses 18.30–32)
  • Fortune $\times$ Fortune: "The product or quotient of two fortunes is a fortune." $$(+) \times (+) = (+)$$
  • Debt $\times$ Debt: "The product or quotient of two debts is a fortune." $$(-) \times (-) = (+)$$
  • Debt $\times$ Fortune: "The product or quotient of a debt and a fortune is a debt." $$(-) \times (+) = (-) \quad\text{and}\quad (+) \times (-) = (-)$$
🏛️ Historic Significance
  • Before Brahmagupta, negative numbers were rejected in many ancient cultures as "absurd" or "impossible quantities."
  • Brahmagupta treated zero ($kha$) and negative numbers as full, legitimate numbers with complete operational arithmetic.
  • This laid the foundational bedrock for global algebra and arithmetic!

Multiplication is Commutative for Integers

Proving that order does not alter magnitude or sign of the product
🔄 Commutative Law for Multiplication: For any two integers $a$ and $b$: $$a \times b = b \times a$$
🔍 Numerical Verification
  • $3 \times (-4) = -12$  and  $(-4) \times 3 = -12$
  • $(-15) \times (-8) = 120$  and  $(-8) \times (-15) = 120$
  • $(-30) \times 12 = -360$  and  $12 \times (-30) = -360$
  • $14 \times (-5) = -70$  and  $(-5) \times 14 = -70$
💡 Why Does It Always Hold?
  • Magnitude Invariance: Magnitude depends purely on the positive product $|a| \times |b|$, which is commutative for whole numbers.
  • Sign Invariance:
    • Both same sign $\implies$ product positive before and after swapping.
    • Different signs $\implies$ product negative before and after swapping.
  • Therefore, neither magnitude nor sign changes!

The Magic Grid of Integers: Multiplication Invariance

Circling 4 numbers with no two in the same row or column always yields the exact same product
🎲 The Game Rules
  • Consider the $4 \times 4$ integer grid:
    Row 1: $[8, -4, 12, -6]$
    Row 2: $[-28, 14, -42, 21]$
    Row 3: $[12, -6, 18, -9]$
    Row 4: $[20, -10, 30, -15]$
  • 1. Circle any unstruck number.
  • 2. Strike out its entire row and column.
  • 3. Repeat until $4$ numbers are circled.
  • 4. Multiply all $4$ circled numbers!
✨ The Underlying Mathematical Secret
  • Every cell $(i, j)$ in the grid is actually the outer product of a row factor and a column factor:
    • Row factors: $[2, -7, 3, 5]$
    • Column factors: $[4, -2, 6, -3]$
  • Notice: $2 \times 4 = 8$, $2 \times (-2) = -4$, $(-7) \times 6 = -42$, etc.!
  • Choosing one from each row and column multiplies ALL row and column factors: $$\text{Product} = (2 \times -7 \times 3 \times 5) \times (4 \times -2 \times 6 \times -3)$$ $$= (-210) \times (-144) = \mathbf{30,240} \quad\text{(Constant!)}$$

SECTION 2.2 DIVISION OF INTEGERS
PAGE 38–39

Division of Integers: Inverse of Multiplication

Converting quotients into missing-factor questions to deduce division sign rules
🔄 Inverse Multiplication Framing
  • $(-100) \div 25 \implies 25 \times \mathbf{?} = -100$.
    Since $25 \times (-4) = -100$, we have $(-100) \div 25 = \mathbf{-4}$.
  • $(-100) \div (-4) \implies (-4) \times \mathbf{?} = -100$.
    Since $(-4) \times 25 = -100$, we have $(-100) \div (-4) = \mathbf{25}$.
  • $50 \div (-25) \implies (-25) \times \mathbf{?} = 50$.
    Since $(-25) \times (-2) = 50$, we have $50 \div (-25) = \mathbf{-2}$.
📐 The Universal Division Rules ($b \ne 0$)
  • Positive $\div$ Negative: $$a \div (-b) = -(a \div b)$$
  • Negative $\div$ Positive: $$(-a) \div b = -(a \div b)$$
  • Negative $\div$ Negative: $$(-a) \div (-b) = a \div b$$
  • Division by zero ($a \div 0$) is strictly undefined!
$$\text{Same signs } \implies \text{Positive quotient} \quad\Big|\quad \text{Different signs } \implies \text{Negative quotient}$$

Associativity & Sign of Products of Many Integers

Grouping flexibility and the master parity rule for negative factors
🔗 Associative Law for Multiplication: For any integers $a, b, c$: $$a \times (b \times c) = (a \times b) \times c$$
🔍 Grouping $5 \times (-3) \times 4$
  • Way 1: $(5 \times -3) \times 4 = (-15) \times 4 = \mathbf{-60}$
  • Way 2: $5 \times (-3 \times 4) = 5 \times (-12) = \mathbf{-60}$
  • Way 3: $(5 \times 4) \times (-3) = 20 \times (-3) = \mathbf{-60}$
  • Products are identical regardless of grouping and evaluation order!
🔢 Parity Rule for Negative Factors
  • $(-1) \times (-1) = +1$ (2 negatives $\to +$)
  • $(-1) \times (-1) \times (-1) = -1$ (3 negatives $\to -$)
  • $(-1)^4 = +1$ (4 negatives $\to +$)
  • $(-1)^5 = -1$ (5 negatives $\to -$)
  • Universal Parity Law:
    • Even number of negatives $\implies$ Product is Positive.
    • Odd number of negatives $\implies$ Product is Negative.

Distributive Property of Multiplication over Addition

Visual grid layout and algebraic equivalence across all integer signs
📐 Distributive Property: For any integers $a, b, c$: $$a \times (b + c) = (a \times b) + (a \times c)$$
🟢🔴 Rectangular Token Visualization
  • Consider the rectangle of tokens for $4 \times (2 + (-3))$:
  • $4$ rows, each containing $2$ green tokens and $3$ red tokens.
  • Left section: $4$ rows of $2$ greens $= 4 \times 2 = 8$.
  • Right section: $4$ rows of $3$ reds $= 4 \times (-3) = -12$.
  • Total tokens $= 8 + (-12) = -4$.
  • LHS: $4 \times (2 - 3) = 4 \times (-1) = -4$ ✅ Matches!
⚡ Mental Math Multiplication Power
  • The distributive property enables rapid mental calculations:
  • Example: $(-5) \times (18 + (-3))$ $$= (-5) \times 15 = -75$$ $$\text{or } (-5 \times 18) + (-5 \times -3) = -90 + 15 = \mathbf{-75}$$
  • Also works over subtraction: $a \times (b - c) = ab - ac$.

Puzzle Time: Terhüchü — The Traditional Game

Ancient strategic grid game from Northeast India played with coins and geometric diagonals
🎲 The Game Setup & Board
  • Origin: Played in Nagaland and Assam, traditionally drawn on large stones or etched in soil.
  • The Grid: $16$ squares with full criss-cross diagonals and $4$ outer triangular corners.
  • Pieces: $2$ players, each commanding $9$ distinct coins placed at opposite sides.
⚔️ Rules of Engagement
  • Movement: Move one coin along any line to an adjacent vacant intersection.
  • Jumping Capture: Jump over an opponent's adjacent coin into a vacant space directly beyond to capture and remove it.
  • Multiple Captures: Continuous jumping turns allowed with directional changes.
  • Triangular Corners: Coins can skip empty intersections inside outer triangular sanctuaries.
  • Objective: Capture all opponent coins or immobilize them!
🏛️ Cultural Math Heritage: Indian indigenous board games embody deep topological line networks, parity rules, and spatial problem-solving.

Master Operations with Integers Reference Checklist

Comprehensive recap of definitions, sign laws, and operational properties
Operations & Sign Rules
  • ✅ Multiplication Signs: Same signs give positive ($+ \cdot + = +$, $- \cdot - = +$). Different signs give negative ($+ \cdot - = -$, $- \cdot + = -$).
  • ✅ Division Signs: Same signs give positive ($+ \div + = +$, $- \div - = +$). Different signs give negative ($+ \div - = -$, $- \div + = -$).
  • ✅ Sign Parity Rule: An even number of negative factors yields positive; an odd number yields negative.
  • ✅ Additive Inverse: $a - b = a + (-b)$. $-(-a) = a$.
  • ✅ Division by Zero: Undefined for all integers.
Fundamental Algebraic Properties
  • ✅ Commutative Property: $a \times b = b \times a$ (Valid for $+$ and $\times$, NOT for $-$ or $\div$).
  • ✅ Associative Property: $(a \times b) \times c = a \times (b \times c)$ (Valid for $+$ and $\times$).
  • ✅ Distributive Property: $a \times (b + c) = (a \times b) + (a \times c)$ over addition and subtraction.
  • ✅ Multiplicative Identity: $1 \times a = a$. Negation: $-1 \times a = -a$.
  • ✅ Brahmagupta's Legacy (628 CE): Fortunes, debts, and zero arithmetic established over 1400 years ago.
